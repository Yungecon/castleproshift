Reading the list

What the wine list taught the system

The wine list is running a second register the memo set does not have, and it is internally consistent enough to codify rather than reconcile. Three things it does that the memo does not.

One. Weight inverts.

The page title sets at 400 caps, large. Bold appears only at line level, on the producer. Big is not bold here. That is the opposite of the memo, where the display carries 700 and the body runs 400. Both are correct, scoped by document type, and the split is worth keeping rather than flattening.

Two. The head rule replaces the accent rule.

Instead of a 46 by 3 bar under the heading, the section label sits inline with a clay hairline that runs from the label to the right margin. It does the same job, marking a section start, but it also measures the column, which a fixed 46px bar cannot do on a list page.

Three. The entry line is a three-step ramp.

Producer at 700 ink, cuvée at 400 italic ink, origin and vintage at 400 mute. Read left to right, the line goes from most to least load-bearing, and it does it with weight, slope, and value instead of size. Every line on 44 pages holds that grammar without a single divider rule.

Note the italic here is black, not clay. In this register italic is a structural slot, not a voice. Clay italic stays reserved for the one editorial aside per page.
